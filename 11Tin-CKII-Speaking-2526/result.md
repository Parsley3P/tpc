### UNIT 6: Cultural Heritage

#### 1. Think of a form of cultural heritage, and discuss ways to preserve it.

> I’d like to talk about traditional Vietnamese Water Puppetry, which is a unique and fascinating form of cultural heritage that dates back to the 11th century. Originally, it was performed in flooded rice paddies in the Red River Delta, and it beautifully captures the daily life and folklore of Vietnamese farmers. 
>
> To preserve this invaluable art form, I believe a multi-faceted approach is necessary. First and foremost, we need to introduce it into the educational curriculum. If schools organize field trips to water puppetry theaters or invite artisans to give workshops, the younger generation will develop an appreciation for it from an early age. 
> 
> Furthermore, the government should provide financial subsidies to the traditional artisans. Many of these performers struggle to make ends meet, and without financial support, they might be forced to abandon their craft for more lucrative jobs. Finally, we can modernize its promotion by integrating it with international tourism. By offering multi-lingual audio guides and using social media campaigns, we can introduce this heritage to a global audience, ensuring it not only survives but thrives in the modern world.

**Noteworthy Grammar Structures:**
* **Relative Clauses:** "...which is a unique and fascinating form..." / "...who struggle to make ends meet..."
* **First Conditional:** "If schools organize field trips... the younger generation will develop..."
* **Participle Clauses:** "By offering multi-lingual audio guides..."

**Noteworthy Vocabulary & Collocations:**
* **Invaluable art form:** Extremely useful/precious type of art.
* **Multi-faceted approach:** A method involving many different aspects or features.
* **Make ends meet:** To earn just enough money to live on (idiom).
* **Lucrative:** Producing a great deal of profit.
* **Not only survives but thrives:** A natural-sounding parallel phrase emphasizing growth.

#### 2. Talk about the problems that cultural heritage faces and possible solutions.

> When discussing the preservation of cultural heritage, it’s impossible to ignore the severe challenges it currently faces. The most pressing issue is rapid globalization. With the heavy influx of Western pop culture, movies, and music, young people are becoming increasingly disconnected from their roots. Traditional festivals and crafts are often viewed as outdated or irrelevant. Additionally, there is a severe lack of funding. Maintaining historical sites or funding traditional art programs is incredibly expensive, and heritage conservation often takes a backseat to economic development.
>
> To tackle these problems, I think the most effective solution is to breathe new life into our heritage by merging it with contemporary culture. For instance, traditional music elements can be mixed into modern pop songs, or historical events can be adapted into high-budget cinematic films. This makes the culture more palatable and engaging for the youth. As for the financial aspect, governments could partner with private corporations. By offering tax incentives to companies that sponsor heritage projects, we can secure the necessary funding without relying solely on the state budget.

**Noteworthy Grammar Structures:**
* **Passive Voice:** "...are often viewed as outdated..." / "...can be mixed into..."
* **Gerunds as Subjects:** "Maintaining historical sites or funding traditional art programs is incredibly expensive..."
* **Infinitive of Purpose:** "To tackle these problems, I think the most effective solution is to..."

**Noteworthy Vocabulary & Collocations:**
* **Pressing issue:** An urgent problem.
* **Influx:** An arrival or entry of large numbers of things.
* **Take a backseat:** To be given lower priority (idiom).
* **Breathe new life into:** To bring new ideas and energy to something (idiom).
* **Palatable:** Acceptable or satisfactory (often used for tastes, but figuratively for ideas).

### UNIT 7: Further Education

#### 3. Which option will be more appropriate for you after leaving school: academic study or vocational training? Why?

> Personally, after leaving high school, I believe pursuing academic study at a university is the more appropriate path for me. While I completely understand the practical appeal of vocational training, my career goal is to become a software engineer, which requires a deep, theoretical foundation in computer science that only a university degree can provide.
>
> One of the main reasons I favor academic study is the broader career trajectory it offers. University doesn't just teach you how to do a specific job; it develops your critical thinking, problem-solving skills, and adaptability. In a rapidly changing tech landscape, these soft skills are crucial. Moreover, university environments provide incredible networking opportunities. Interacting with professors, joining academic clubs, and meeting like-minded peers can open doors to internships and collaborations that are essential for long-term career growth. Even though it requires a significant investment of time and money—typically four years—I view it as a necessary stepping stone to achieve my professional ambitions.

**Noteworthy Grammar Structures:**
* **Concession Clauses:** "While I completely understand the practical appeal..." / "Even though it requires a significant investment..."
* **Non-defining Relative Clause:** "...my career goal is to become a software engineer, which requires..."
* **Parallel Structure:** "...develops your critical thinking, problem-solving skills, and adaptability."

**Noteworthy Vocabulary & Collocations:**
* **Theoretical foundation:** The underlying concepts and principles of a subject.
* **Career trajectory:** The path one's career takes over time.
* **Soft skills:** Personal attributes that enable someone to interact effectively and harmoniously with others.
* **Like-minded peers:** People who share similar interests and opinions.
* **Stepping stone:** An action or event that helps one to make progress towards a specified goal.

#### 4. What factors influence school leavers when choosing their future paths/ careers?

> Choosing a future career path is arguably one of the most critical decisions a young person makes, and it is usually influenced by a combination of factors. In my observation, the most dominant factor—especially in Asian cultures—is parental expectation. Many parents want their children to pursue prestigious and stable careers, like medicine or law, which can place immense pressure on school leavers to conform to their family’s wishes rather than following their own passions.
>
> Another major factor is the current state of the job market and financial prospects. Let's face it, economic reality hits hard. Students are naturally drawn to fields that offer a high rate of employability and a lucrative salary upon graduation, such as information technology or digital marketing. Lastly, a student’s innate talent and intrinsic motivation play a crucial role. A person who is naturally gifted in the arts will likely gravitate towards design or music, despite the potential financial instability. Ultimately, the choice is usually a balancing act between passion, practicality, and family influence.

**Noteworthy Grammar Structures:**
* **Adverbial Modifiers:** "arguably one of the most critical..." / "naturally drawn to..."
* **Noun Clauses:** "...what influences school leavers is usually..."
* **Participial Phrases:** "...following their own passions."

**Noteworthy Vocabulary & Collocations:**
* **Dominant factor:** The most important or influential element.
* **Conform to:** To comply with rules, standards, or laws.
* **Lucrative salary:** A very profitable or well-paying wage.
* **Intrinsic motivation:** Doing an activity for its inherent satisfaction rather than for some separable consequence.
* **Balancing act:** An attempt to cope with several often conflicting factors or situations at the same time.

### UNIT 8: Independence

#### 5. What skills do you think teenagers need to become independent?

> If teenagers want to successfully stand on their own two feet, there is a specific skill set they need to master before leaving the nest. First and foremost is financial literacy. Teenagers must understand how to create a basic budget, track their expenses, and distinguish between needs and wants. Without this skill, they can easily fall into debt the moment they start living alone.
>
> Secondly, basic domestic skills are non-negotiable. I'm talking about knowing how to cook healthy meals, do laundry, and maintain a clean living space. Relying on takeout food is not only financially draining but also terrible for one's health. Finally, I would argue that emotional resilience is just as critical as practical skills. When you become independent, you no longer have your parents there to shield you from every problem. Teenagers need to learn how to manage stress, cope with loneliness, and solve day-to-day issues proactively rather than panicking. Mastering these areas creates a solid foundation for adulthood.

**Noteworthy Grammar Structures:**
* **Conditional (Zero/First):** "If teenagers want to... there is a specific skill set..."
* **Comparative Structures:** "...just as critical as..."
* **Negative Inversion (implied):** "Not only financially draining but also terrible..."

**Noteworthy Vocabulary & Collocations:**
* **Stand on their own two feet:** To be independent and self-sufficient (idiom).
* **Leave the nest:** To move out of one's parents' home (idiom).
* **Financial literacy:** The ability to understand and effectively use various financial skills.
* **Non-negotiable:** Not open to discussion or modification.
* **Emotional resilience:** The ability to adapt to stressful situations or crises.

#### 6. Talk about some problems you face when becoming independent and how to overcome them.

> Stepping into independence is an exciting journey, but it is certainly fraught with challenges. Speaking from personal observation, the most immediate hurdle is usually financial strain. When you are suddenly responsible for rent, groceries, and utility bills, money seems to vanish instantly. To overcome this, strict budgeting is absolutely essential. I highly recommend using financial tracking apps and perhaps taking on a part-time job to ensure a steady stream of income without compromising academic or professional duties.
>
> Another significant problem is the feeling of isolation. When you move out of your family home, the sudden quietness can be quite overwhelming. You have to handle illness, bad days, and stress entirely on your own. The best way to combat this homesickness and isolation is to proactively build a new support system. This means stepping out of your comfort zone to join local clubs, volunteering, or simply making an effort to socialize with neighbors or roommates. Staying in regular virtual contact with family also helps ease the transition from dependence to full self-reliance.

**Noteworthy Grammar Structures:**
* **Adjective + Infinitive:** "...exciting journey, but it is certainly fraught with challenges."
* **Time Clauses:** "When you are suddenly responsible... money seems to vanish..."
* **Gerunds after Prepositions:** "...without compromising academic or professional duties."

**Noteworthy Vocabulary & Collocations:**
* **Fraught with:** Filled with (something undesirable).
* **Financial strain:** Pressure on one's financial resources.
* **Steady stream of income:** A reliable and continuous earning of money.
* **Overwhelming:** Very great in amount; having a strong emotional effect.
* **Support system:** A network of people who provide practical or emotional support.

### UNIT 9: Social Issues

#### 7. What are some social problems that teenagers in Vietnam face?

> Teenagers in Vietnam today are growing up in a rapidly changing environment, which exposes them to several unique social problems. The most prominent issue, without a doubt, is intense academic pressure. The Vietnamese educational system is highly competitive, and there is a pervasive societal belief that getting into a prestigious university is the only path to a successful life. Consequently, teenagers are burdened with overwhelming amounts of homework and extra tutoring classes, which takes a massive toll on their mental health, leading to anxiety and sleep deprivation.
>
> Another escalating problem is the rise of cyberbullying. With smartphones and social media becoming ubiquitous among the youth, peer conflicts have moved from the schoolyard to the digital world. Teenagers can now be harassed anonymously online 24/7. This constant exposure to unrealistic social media standards and online toxicity often results in body image issues and a severe drop in self-esteem. It's a very challenging landscape to navigate compared to previous generations.

**Noteworthy Grammar Structures:**
* **Non-defining Relative Clause:** "...rapidly changing environment, which exposes them to..."
* **Noun Clauses as Subjects:** "...there is a pervasive societal belief that getting into a prestigious university is the only path..."
* **Cause and Effect phrasing:** "Consequently...", "This constant exposure... results in..."

**Noteworthy Vocabulary & Collocations:**
* **Prominent issue:** A very noticeable or important problem.
* **Pervasive:** Spreading widely throughout an area or a group of people.
* **Take a massive toll on:** To cause damage or suffering over time (idiom).
* **Ubiquitous:** Present, appearing, or found everywhere.
* **Online toxicity:** Negative, abusive, or harmful behavior on the internet.

#### 8. Talk about some negative effects of peer pressure and possible solutions.

> Peer pressure is a powerful force during adolescence, and its negative effects can be quite damaging. When teenagers are desperate to fit in with a certain crowd, they often adopt a 'herd mentality.' This means they might succumb to risky behaviors, such as smoking, skipping classes, or underage drinking, simply to gain validation from their peers. Furthermore, intense peer pressure can strip away a teenager's individuality; they might suppress their true interests and talents just because those traits aren't considered "cool" by their social group.
>
> To mitigate these issues, we need to focus on building self-worth. The most effective solution starts at home. Parents should foster an environment of open, non-judgmental communication so teenagers feel comfortable discussing their social struggles. Additionally, schools should implement assertiveness training programs. If we teach adolescents how to confidently say "no" and how to recognize toxic friendships, they will be much better equipped to stand their ground. Ultimately, a teenager with high self-esteem is far less likely to cave under the pressure of their peers.

**Noteworthy Grammar Structures:**
* **Adverbial Clauses of Time/Condition:** "When teenagers are desperate to fit in..."
* **Modal Verbs for Recommendation:** "...schools should implement...", "Parents should foster..."
* **Comparative Adjectives:** "...much better equipped...", "...far less likely to..."

**Noteworthy Vocabulary & Collocations:**
* **Herd mentality:** The tendency for people's behavior to conform to those of the group to which they belong.
* **Succumb to:** Fail to resist pressure, temptation, or some other negative force.
* **Validation:** Recognition or affirmation that a person or their feelings are valid or worthwhile.
* **Mitigate:** Make less severe, serious, or painful.
* **Stand their ground:** To refuse to be pushed backwards, or to continue in your beliefs in an argument (idiom).
